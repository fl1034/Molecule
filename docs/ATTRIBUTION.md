### 1EMA — Green fluorescent protein

Crystal structure of the Aequorea victoria green fluorescent protein. (1996). https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1EMA

Verified selections: Protein fold · A; Chromophore · A66; Arginine · A96. Author chain/residue identifiers are used, with no insertion codes in the detailed selections.

The colors identify components; they do not simulate fluorescence. This entry contains selenomethionine. Model 1 · deposited coordinates.

Reference packet: Green fluorescent protein from the jellyfish Aequorea victoria encloses a chromophore inside a barrel-like protein fold. This is a coordinate model, not a picture of emitted light. The modified residue CRO at author position A66 is the light-absorbing and light-emitting center. Isolate its atoms to see a structure that differs from an ordinary amino acid. Restore the surrounding fold and inspect arginine A96 alongside the chromophore. These are specific residues in the deposited model; no reaction is being animated. Return to the whole fold. The protein surrounds its chromophore, an arrangement that underlies GFP’s usefulness as a fluorescent marker in biological research.

### 1LYZ — Lysozyme

Real-space refinement of the structure of hen egg-white lysozyme. (1974). https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1LYZ

Primary paper: https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-2836(74)90598-1

Verified selections: Lysozyme · A; Glutamate · A35; Aspartate · A52. Author chain/residue identifiers are used, with no insertion codes in the detailed selections.

No sugar substrate is present in this entry. Model 1 · deposited coordinates.

Reference packet: Hen egg-white lysozyme is an enzyme that breaks bonds in bacterial cell-wall carbohydrates. This structure shows the protein alone, without a bound sugar substrate. Locate glutamate A35, one of the residues associated with lysozyme’s catalytic site. Its atoms are shown as sticks against the folded protein. Isolate glutamate A35 and aspartate A52 together. Both are present in the experimental coordinates; this view highlights their positions without inventing a substrate or a reaction. Restore the whole enzyme around the highlighted residues. The active site is part of a larger three-dimensional protein, not a separate molecular component.

### 1EHZ — Transfer RNA

The crystal structure of yeast phenylalanine tRNA at 1.93 A resolution: a classic structure revisited (2000). https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1EHZ

Primary paper: https://doi.org/10.1017/S1355838200000364

Verified selections: tRNA · A; Anticodon · A34–36; CCA end · A74–76. Author chain/residue identifiers are used, with no insertion codes in the detailed selections.

Yeast phenylalanine tRNA; no attached amino acid or ribosome is shown. Model 1 · deposited coordinates.

Reference packet: This yeast phenylalanine transfer RNA folds into a compact three-dimensional shape. Transfer RNAs connect the genetic code to amino acids during protein synthesis. The anticodon occupies author residues 34–36. This structure includes a modified guanosine at position 34, followed by adenine and adenine. The CCA sequence at residues 74–76 is at the amino-acid attachment end of tRNA. No amino acid is attached in the displayed model. Restore the RNA with both regions marked. The anticodon and the CCA end occupy different regions of the folded molecule; a ribosome is not part of this entry.

### 1MBN — Myoglobin

The Stereochemistry of the Protein Myoglobin (1969). https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1MBN

Verified selections: Myoglobin · A; Heme · A155. Author chain/residue identifiers are used, with no insertion codes in the detailed selections.

A historic sperm-whale myoglobin model. The heme is shown; no bound O2 molecule is added. Model 1 · deposited coordinates.

Reference packet: Myoglobin is an oxygen-binding protein found in muscle. Unlike the four-chain hemoglobin example, this deposited model has one protein chain around a heme group. Author residue A155 is the heme. Its ring surrounds an iron atom, providing a familiar chemical motif shared with hemoglobin. Restore the protein around the selected heme. The ribbon traces the folded protein backbone, while the sticks show the heme’s atomic bonds. Return to the whole myoglobin fold, then compare it with hemoglobin in the collection. Similar cofactors can appear in proteins with different chain arrangements and biological roles.

### 1F88 — Rhodopsin

Crystal structure of rhodopsin: A G protein-coupled receptor. (2000). https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1F88

Primary paper: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.289.5480.739

Verified selections: Receptor copy · A; Receptor copy · B; Retinal · A977. Author chain/residue identifiers are used, with no insertion codes in the detailed selections.

Bovine rhodopsin crystal model. No membrane or light-triggered transition is simulated; glycans and crystallization additives are omitted from the tours. Model 1 · deposited coordinates.

Reference packet: Rhodopsin is a light-sensitive G protein-coupled receptor. This deposited crystal model contains two receptor copies; the two copies do not by themselves establish a functional dimer. Isolate author chain A. Its helical fold surrounds the retinal-binding region. A lipid membrane is not included in this scene. Retinal is the small light-sensitive molecule in the receptor. This selection is author residue A977, identified as RET in the file. The static coordinates do not show photoisomerization. Restore chain A around retinal to see the relationship between the small chromophore and the much larger receptor.

### 1BMF — ATP synthase: the F1 head

Structure at 2.8 A resolution of F1-ATPase from bovine heart mitochondria. (1994). https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1BMF

Primary paper: https://doi.org/10.1038/370621a0

Verified selections: Alpha subunits · A–C; Beta subunits · D–F; Gamma subunit · G; ADP and ANP. Author chain/residue identifiers are used, with no insertion codes in the detailed selections.

Only the soluble F1 portion is present, not the membrane motor. ANP is a nucleotide analogue, not ATP. Model 1 · deposited coordinates.

Reference packet: This bovine mitochondrial F1-ATPase structure contains three alpha and three beta subunits around a gamma subunit. It is the soluble portion of ATP synthase, not the complete membrane complex. Isolate the deposited gamma chain G. Its position within the larger complex helps explain why F1 is studied as part of a rotary molecular machine; no rotation is simulated here. The file contains ADP in chain D and the analogue ANP in chains A, B, C, and F. These are distinct chemical identities; the analogue is not labeled as ATP. Restore the full deposited F1 complex. The three beta chains are not identical in their observed conformations or nucleotide occupancy, a central observation in the cited structural study.
